# Policy Brief: Generative AI and Singapore Tech Workforce Trajectory (Conflicting Empirical Perspectives)
**Author:** Ministry of Trade & Industry (MTI) Policy Review Group
**Publication Date:** June 2024
**Classification:** Policy Research Working Paper (Public Release)
**Doc ID:** SG-WORKFORCE-DISPUTE-2024

## 1. Context of the Debate
The rapid diffusion of generative AI and automated code synthesis tools has sparked intense debate among economic agencies and labour policy bodies regarding the net impact on Singapore's infocomm workforce between 2024 and 2028. Two distinct, empirical schools of thought have published divergent projections.

## 2. Perspective A: The IMDA / Economic Development Board (EDB) Pro-Growth Synthesis
The IMDA and EDB Joint Economic Analysis (May 2024) models generative AI as a powerful net job creator:
- **Projected Net Job Creation:** Generative AI is projected to generate **22,000 net new hybrid tech jobs** in Singapore by 2027, primarily in AI engineering, prompt architecture, AI governance audit, and specialized systems integration.
- **Augmentation vs Displacement:** The report finds that **78% of existing PMET functions** will experience task augmentation rather than full job obsolescence, leading to an estimated 14% uplift in labour productivity across the tech sector.
- **Wage Outlook:** Real median tech wages are forecasted to grow by 4.5% to 6.0% annually, underpinned by strong corporate demand for AI-literate talent.
- **Empirical Baseline:** Derived from enterprise survey data across 1,200 tech firms and historical automation elasticity coefficients from SingStat 2018-2023.

## 3. Perspective B: The Independent Labour Research Institute (ILRI) / Union Advisory Warning
In contrast, the Independent Labour Research Institute (ILRI) published an alternative econometric simulation in June 2024:
- **Projected Net Disruption:** Generative AI is projected to cause **net displacement of up to 18,500 roles** by 2027, predominantly affecting entry-level software developers, junior quality assurance testers, level-1 helpdesk personnel, and routine back-office data clerks.
- **Junior Hiring Cliff:** ILRI reports that fresh graduate tech hiring quotas at leading IT consulting firms declined by 26% year-on-year in 2023 as automated code generation tools reduced entry-level task demand.
- **Wage Bifurcation:** The study forecasts severe wage bifurcation: while senior AI architects will see wage premiums, junior software engineers face median wage stagnation (+0.8% annually) or real wage depreciation.
- **Empirical Baseline:** Derived from micro-level job vacancy scraping across 45,000 public tech postings and task-replacibility indices calibrated on frontier LLM benchmark capabilities.

## 4. Policy Reconciling Analysis for Researchers
When synthesizing tech workforce trajectories:
1. **Source Discrepancy Note:** The IMDA/EDB model assumes rapid industry re-absorption and high government training subsidy uptake, whereas the ILRI model highlights frictional transition costs, structural mismatch, and delayed hiring transitions.
2. **Analytical Guidance:** Policy researchers MUST NOT present either projection as an uncontested consensus. Any policy advisory must explicitly juxtapose the optimistic +22,000 job creation thesis against the cautious -18,500 role displacement vulnerability, identifying key conditionality (such as training subsidy uptake and SME adoption rates).
