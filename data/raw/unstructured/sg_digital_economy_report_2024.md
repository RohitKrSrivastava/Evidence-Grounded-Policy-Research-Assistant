# Singapore Digital Economy Report 2024
**Author:** Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA)
**Publication Date:** January 2024
**Classification:** Open / Public Release
**Doc ID:** SG-DER-2024

## 1. Executive Summary
The Singapore digital economy has continued its robust post-pandemic trajectory, demonstrating double-digit resilience despite global macroeconomic headwinds. In 2023, the digital economy contributed an estimated 17.7% of Singapore's Gross Domestic Product (GDP), up from 13.0% in 2018. This expansion reflects both the expansion of the Infocomm Media (ICM) sector and the rapid digitalization of traditional industries, including retail, logistics, manufacturing, and financial services.

## 2. Macroeconomic Contribution of the ICM Sector
The Infocomm Media sector generated SGD 38.6 billion in Value Added (VA) in 2023, registering a compounded annual expansion of over 14% since 2018, when sector VA stood at SGD 19.2 billion. Gross operating revenues exceeded SGD 128.9 billion in 2023. Growth drivers included cross-border cloud architecture, SaaS enterprise platforms, digital advertising, and enterprise artificial intelligence deployments. Singapore retains its status as the regional digital hub, housing Asia-Pacific operational headquarters for 59 of the world's top 100 technology corporations.

## 3. The Artificial Intelligence Frontier: Enterprise Adoption
Enterprise adoption of artificial intelligence reached 44.0% across all business sizes in 2023, surging from 14.2% in 2020. The primary catalyst was the emergence of generative AI and accessible Large Language Model (LLM) APIs, which lowered technical onboarding barriers for small and medium enterprises (SMEs). However, a clear adoption gap persists between multinational corporations (78.5% adoption) and local small businesses (28.2% adoption), driven primarily by data readiness, integration overhead, and talent constraints.

## 4. SME Digitalisation and Capability Building
Through IMDA's SME Go Digital programme, over 90,000 local businesses have adopted pre-approved digital solutions since inception. In 2023 alone, the CTO-as-a-Service initiative provided tailored digital diagnostic advice to more than 6,000 SMEs. Grant disbursements under the Productivity Solutions Grant (PSG) reached 21,250 approvals across target sectors, including F&B, retail, and precision engineering. Survey findings show that 85% of participating SMEs reported productivity gains of at least 15% within 12 months of adopting digital workflow automation.

## 5. Infrastructure Resilience: Connectivity and Green Compute
To power the next bound of digital compute, Singapore established the Green Data Centre Roadmap, targeting 1,400 megawatts of energy-efficient operational data centre capacity by 2030 while maintaining a strict Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) cap of 1.3 for all new installations. Furthermore, Singapore expanded its subsea cable connectivity, with 26 subsea cables landing across 4 international cable landing stations, reinforcing Singapore's ultra-low-latency backbone for generative AI clusters and cloud interconnects.

## 6. Strategic Policy Recommendations for FY2025-2030
1. **Targeted AI Reskilling Subsidies:** Increase SkillsFuture Enterprise Credit matching for AI literacy and prompt engineering courses.
2. **Data Commons Architecture:** Establish trusted sector-specific data sharing federations to overcome SME data scarcity.
3. **Regulatory Sandboxing for Agentic AI:** Provide controlled regulatory testbeds for autonomous AI workflows in sensitive sectors, ensuring liability and audit trails are preserved.
